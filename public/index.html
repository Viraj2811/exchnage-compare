How the comparison works

Instead of showing a single headline price, Priceboard downloads each exchange's live order book, which is the list of real buy and sell orders. It then simulates your trade against those orders:

  1. Your order uses the best prices first and moves to worse ones as they run out.
  2. The exchange's trading fee is taken off what you receive.
  3. Exchanges are ranked by what you end up with, coins when buying or money when selling.

Small orders mostly depend on the spread. Large orders depend on depth, so the best exchange can change as you change the amount.

Frequently asked questions

Which exchange has the best price for Bitcoin right now?

It changes every few seconds. This tool reads live order books from several exchanges and ranks them by how much you actually get for your amount, after trading fees.

Why does the price depend on how much I trade?

Each exchange has a stack of buy and sell orders at different prices. A larger order uses up the best orders and moves on to worse ones, so your average price gets worse. This is called slippage, and the tool calculates it from the real order book.

Why do some exchanges show USDT instead of USD?

Many global exchanges trade against the stablecoin USDT, which is usually worth about one US dollar but not exactly. Those rows are labeled with their quote currency so you can compare fairly.

Are the fees correct for my account?

Fees are starting estimates for the lowest trading tier. Your own rate may be lower. Edit any fee in the Fee settings panel and the ranking updates instantly.
